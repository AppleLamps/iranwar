## Toplines

**Israeli and US officials told Axios on March 13 that Israel is planning to “significantly expand” its ground campaign in Lebanon to dismantle Hezbollah infrastructure south of the Litani River.[1]** A senior Israeli official said that Israel’s objectives are to “take over territory, push Hezbollah’s forces north and away from the border, and dismantle [Hezbollah’s] military positions and weapons depots in [southern Lebanese] villages.”[2] Israeli and US officials said that the Israel Defense Forces (IDF) seeks to seize “the entire area” south of the Litani River to dismantle Hezbollah military infrastructure in southern Lebanon.[3] US and Israeli officials said that the United States supports the proposed Israeli operation to disarm Hezbollah.[4] The United States has reportedly asked Israel not to target the Beirut Rafic Hariri International Airport and other Lebanese state infrastructure, however.[5] US officials said that Israel agreed not to target the Beirut airport and would consult with the United States on a “case-by-case basis” about striking other Lebanese state infrastructure.[6] Israeli media reported on March 12 that the Israeli operation in Lebanon “will begin within a week.”[7] Israeli officials told Israeli media on March 12 that Israeli operations could continue in Lebanon for months even if the conflict with Iran ends soon.[8]

**The IDF continues to deploy additional forces to northern Israel to support operations against Hezbollah in Lebanon.** The IDF reported that the IDF Northern Command, which commands IDF units operating on the Israel-Lebanon border, will receive additional forces “during the coming week.”[9] IDF Chief of Staff Lieutenant General Eyal Zamir ordered the deployment of additional forces to the Northern Command on March 12. Zamir stated on March 13 that the reinforcements will include a division, two brigades, and multiple engineering battalions.[10] These reinforcements will supplement the three IDF divisions that have been deployed on the Israel-Lebanon border since the start of the war.[11] Israeli media reported on March 14 that Israeli tank transporters and armored personnel carriers are moving toward the Israel-Lebanon border as part of preparations for a potential “large-scale” IDF ground maneuver.[12]

**Israeli and Lebanese officials are reportedly preparing to hold direct talks mediated by the United States “in the coming days.”[13]** Two sources familiar with the matter told Israeli media that Israeli and Lebanese delegations are expected to meet for direct talks in either Paris or Cyprus “in the coming days.”[14] Senior US and Israeli officials told Axios and Israeli media on March 14 that Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has tasked former Strategic Affairs Minister Ron Dermer with the “Lebanese file” and leading initial Israeli negotiations with the Lebanese government.[15] An unspecified Lebanese official told Qatari media on March 14 that the Lebanese government is preparing to form a negotiating delegation comprised of Lebanese diplomats to discuss de-escalation with Israel.[16] The Lebanese official said that Lebanese Parliament Speaker Nabih Berri agreed not to include a Hezbollah or Amal Movement representative in the delegation.[17] The Lebanese official said that the Lebanese government has called for Israel and Lebanon to reimplement the November 2024 ceasefire agreement before negotiating further.[18] US mediation efforts will reportedly be led by either US Senior Adviser for Arab and African Affairs Massad Boulos or Jared Kushner.[19] Five sources “with knowledge of the matter” told Axios on March 9 that the Lebanese government proposed immediate direct negotiations with Israel under US auspices to reach a peace agreement.[20] An unspecified senior Israeli official told Israeli media on March 11 that Israel had begun to hold indirect talks with the Lebanese government.[21] Israel reportedly demanded that the Lebanese Armed Forces (LAF) act against Hezbollah immediately.[22]

**France has reportedly proposed a peace plan between Israel and Lebanon that would include Lebanon normalizing relations with Israel.[23]** Three sources familiar with the details of the plan told Axios on March 14 that the French government has drafted an Israel-Lebanon peace plan proposal.[24] The proposal includes multiple stages of negotiations that would be mediated by both the United States and France.[25] The negotiations would reportedly begin with negotiations between Israeli and Lebanese diplomats before advancing to talks between Israeli and Lebanese political leaders.[26] The French peace plan proposal includes the following stages:

- **Stage 1:** Lebanon and Israel initiate negotiations and agree to a “political declaration” within one month.[27] The sources stated that Lebanon would agree to respect Israeli sovereignty and territorial integrity as part of the “political declaration.”[28] The declaration would also reportedly include Lebanon’s “initial recognition of Israel.”[29] The Lebanese government would additionally commit to preventing any attacks against Israel from Lebanese territory and disarming Hezbollah.[30] Israel and Lebanon would affirm their commitment to United Nations Security Council (UNSC) Resolution 1701, which ended the 2006 Lebanon War, and the November 2024 ceasefire agreement.[31]
- **Stage 2:** The LAF would redeploy forces south of the Litani River as the IDF withdraws from Lebanese territory that it has seized in the current conflict.[32] Israel and Lebanon would also commit to engaging through the US-led ceasefire monitoring committee to address ceasefire violations during this period.[33] UNIFIL peacekeepers would reportedly verify that the LAF was sufficiently disarming Hezbollah south of the Litani River, while an unspecified UNSC-mandated ”coalition of countries” would oversee Hezbollah’s disarmament throughout the rest of Lebanon.[34]
- **Stage 3:** Lebanon would be required to sign a permanent non-aggression agreement with Israel within two months of signing the “political declaration” in stage one.[35] The non-aggression agreement would formally end the state of war between Israel and Lebanon that has existed between the two countries since 1948.[36]
- **Stage 4:** Israel would withdraw its forces from the five positions in Lebanon that the IDF has occupied since February 2025 over an unspecified period.[37] Lebanon would also be expected to complete negotiations with Israel and Syria to demarcate Lebanon’s borders by the end of 2026.[38]

French President Emmanuel Macron said on March 14 that he recently spoke with Lebanese President Joseph Aoun, Prime Minister Nawaf Salam, and Parliament Speaker Nabih Berri to discuss efforts to halt the conflict in Lebanon. Macron said that Aoun, Berri, and Salam “indicated willingness” to engage in direct talks with Israel. Macron also said that France is willing to host the Israel-Lebanon talks in Paris.[39]

**Neither Iran nor the United States is prepared to hold discussions to reach a ceasefire despite mediation efforts by Oman and Egypt, according to Iranian, regional, and White House officials speaking to Reuters on March 14.[40]** Three sources familiar with the efforts told Reuters that the Trump administration rejected efforts by Middle Eastern allies, including Oman and Egypt, to start diplomatic talks to end the war with Iran.[41] Two senior Iranian officials said that Iran rejected the possibility of a ceasefire until the United States and Israel stop striking Iran and meet Iranian demands, which reportedly include compensation.[42] A third Iranian source stated that previous diplomatic efforts between Iran and the United States are “irrelevant now.”[43] The source added that Islamic Revolutionary Guards Corps (IRGC) officials “strongly believe that if they lose control over the Strait of Hormuz, Iran will lose the war.”[44] A senior White House official told Reuters that President Donald Trump is “not interested in [diplomatic talks] right now.”[45]

## US and Israeli Air Campaign

**The IDF confirmed on March 14 that it killed two senior Iranian Khatam ol Anbia Central Headquarters intelligence officials, Abdollah Jalali Nassab and Amir Shariat, on March 13.[46]** The IDF stated that Nassab and Shariat had replaced Khatam ol Anbia Central Headquarters Intelligence Chief Saleh Asadi, whom Israel targeted and killed in airstrikes on February 28.[47] The Khatam ol Anbia Central Headquarters oversees and coordinates Iranian military activities, serving as the main authority for operational command and control.[48]

**The United States struck Iranian military infrastructure on Kharg Island in Bushehr Province on March 13, in part to degrade Iran’s ability to threaten shipping in the Strait of Hormuz.** US Central Command (CENTCOM) stated that US forces conducted large-scale precision strikes targeting Kharg Island, destroying naval mine storage facilities, missile storage facilities, and other military sites.[49] CENTCOM added that it struck over 90 Iranian military targets on the island.[50] Several open-source intelligence (OSINT) accounts, including “Stinky,” “Pixel,” ”Wooltiger Hunter,” and Mitchell Ulrich, geolocated CENTCOM footage that shows that the United States struck Kharg Airport, the Kharg Air Defense Complex, and a Matla ul Fajr radar on Kharg Island.[51] The Matla ul Fajr radar reportedly has long-range early warning and air surveillance capabilities with a detection range of up to 500 kilometers for identifying and tracking aerial targets.[52] US President Donald Trump stated on March 14 that the US strikes targeted military installations on the island but did not target oil infrastructure.[53] Trump warned that the United States could decide to target oil infrastructure on the island if Iran continues to interfere with shipping through the Strait of Hormuz.[54] CENTCOM previously reported on March 10 that US forces had destroyed 16 Iranian minelayers near the Strait of Hormuz.[55] Iran has so far deployed fewer than 10 naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz.[56] A 2025 US Congressional Report said that Iran has 5,000-6,000 naval mines.[57]

**The combined force has continued to target Iranian industrial zones, which often contain companies tied to Iran’s defense industrial base and the Ministry of Defense and Armed Forces Logistics (MODAFL).** The combined force struck the Jey Industrial Zone in Esfahan Province on March 14 after previously targeting the area on March 6.[58] The Jey Industrial Zone contains facilities tied to Iranian drone manufacturing and defense industrial activities.[59] The United States previously sanctioned Kimia Part Sivan Company, which is located in the Jey Industrial Zone, for supporting the IRGC Quds Force.[60] US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth stated on March 13 that US and Israeli strikes have “functionally defeated” Iran’s ballistic missile production capability by destroying defense companies and production lines across Iran that manufacture missile components.[61]

**The Israel Defense Forces (IDF) struck the Iranian Space Research Center in Tehran City on March 14, likely to disrupt Iranian space and missile-related research that could support long-range strike capabilities.[62]** The Iranian Space Research Center is a subsidiary of the Iranian Space Agency and operates under Iran’s Ministry of Information and Communications Technology.[63] The United States sanctioned the Iranian Space Research Center in September 2019 for supporting Iran’s space launch vehicle program.[64] The US Treasury Department stated in 2019 that the Iranian Space Research Center conducts research and development activities for the Iranian Space Agency and has worked with the UN-designated liquid propellant ballistic missile organization Shahid Hemmat Industrial Group.[65] The US Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA) has assessed that Iran’s space launch vehicle program could enable the regime to develop a militarily viable intercontinental ballistic missile by 2035 if it chose to pursue the capability, which highlights US concerns about the overlap between Iran’s space launch vehicle program and intercontinental ballistic missile development.[66]

**The combined force has continued to target Iranian internal security institutions in Tehran that the regime previously used to suppress protests.** The combined force struck Law Enforcement Command (LEC) Station 108 in the Navab neighborhood of Tehran and LEC Station 148 in the Enghelab neighborhood of Tehran on March 14.[67] The LEC plays a central role in regime repression and internal security operations, including suppressing protests.[68] Iranian security forces previously used several LEC stations in Tehran, including those in Navab and other neighborhoods, to respond to and suppress protest activity during the December 2025-January 2026 protests.[69] These strikes, therefore, likely seek to degrade the regime’s internal security apparatus and its ability to quell unrest. Satellite imagery from March 6 also confirms that the combined force destroyed an intelligence office in Sarpol-e Zahab, Kermanshah Province.[70]

**The IDF has continued to degrade Iran’s air defense network.** The IDF reported on March 14 that it struck sites used to produce air defense systems west of Karaj, Alborz Province, and in western Tehran.[71] US Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth stated on March 13 that “Iran has no air defenses.”[72]

The combined force also struck several Artesh and IRGC bases on March 13 and 14, including the Artesh Air Force 7th Tactical Airbase in Shiraz, Fars Province, the IRGC Navy Shahid Asari Nejad Barracks in Shiraz, a naval base in Sirjan, Kerman Province, and the 4th Artesh Ground Forces Aviation Base in Esfahan Province.[73]

## Iranian Response

**Iran has continued to attack Israel and the Gulf states.** Iran has conducted seven missile attacks against Israel since ISW-CTP’s last data cutoff at 3:00 PM ET on March 13.[74] An Iranian cluster munition impacted in Eilat on March 14, wounding three people.[75] An Iranian cluster munition also impacted in central Israel on March 14.[76] The Saudi Defense Ministry announced on March 14 that Saudi air defenses had intercepted 20 drones and one ballistic missile since March 13.[77] The Emirati Defense Ministry separately announced that Emirati air defenses engaged 33 Iranian drones and nine ballistic missiles on March 14.[78] Unspecified industry sources told Western media on March 14 that Iran conducted a drone attack targeting Fujairah Port in the United Arab Emirates (UAE) on March 14, which Emirati air defenses intercepted.[79] The sources stated that oil operations at Fujairah Port were suspended after debris from the interception caused a fire at the port.[80]

**Iran conducted drone attacks targeting Citibank buildings in Dubai, the United Arab Emirates (UAE), and Manama, Bahrain, on March 13 in retaliation for a combined force strike on an Iranian state-run bank on March 11.[81]** Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi told Western media on March 14 that Iran responded “in kind” to the combined force’s strike on a Bank Sepah building on March 11.[82]

**United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) reported on March 14 that it has not observed any confirmed vessel attacks in the Persian Gulf, Strait of Hormuz, and Gulf of Oman in the past 48 hours.[83]** UKMTO warned that the threat environment in these areas remains critical, however.[84] UKMTO stated that it has observed 20 confirmed maritime incidents involving commercial vessels and offshore infrastructure since March 1.[85] UKMTO stated that Iran has not consistently targeted vessels with Western owners, which it assessed suggests that Iran is conducting a broader campaign aimed at “broad maritime disruption rather than selective vessel targeting.”[86] UKMTO also reported that traffic through the Strait of Hormuz has sharply declined, noting that around 138 vessels usually transit through the strait per day, but that currently only around two vessels are transiting through the strait per day.[87] US President Donald Trump stated on March 14 that multiple countries affected by disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz, including China, France, Japan, South Korea, and the United Kingdom, will “hopefully” deploy warships alongside the United States to protect international shipping.[88]

## Israeli Campaign Against Hezbollah and Hezbollah Response

**Hezbollah claimed 43 attacks targeting Israeli forces and positions in northern Israel and southern Lebanon, as well as northern Israeli towns, between 3:00 PM ET on March 13 and 3:00 PM ET on March 14.[89] This marks the highest number of attacks that Hezbollah has claimed in a 24-hour period since the start of the war.** Hezbollah said that it launched seven rocket attacks at northern Israeli communities.[90] The IDF reported on March 14 that Hezbollah has launched an average of around 100 rockets per day targeting Israel since March 2.[91] Several drones fired from Lebanon were either intercepted by the IDF or landed in open areas in northern Israel on March 14, causing no damage or casualties.[92] Israeli Army Radio reported that rockets fired from Lebanon fell in open areas in Krayot, causing no casualties.[93] Hezbollah claimed a drone attack targeting IDF soldiers at a “rehabilitation and maintenance center” south of Haifa.[94] Hezbollah also claimed that it conducted three rocket and missile attacks targeting IDF sites in northern Israel and engaged two Israeli Merkava tanks at the Jal al Alam site, which is just over the Blue Line that demarcates the Israel-Lebanon border.[95]

Hezbollah claimed that it conducted seven rocket attacks and a missile attack targeting Israeli forces in Lebanon.[96] Hezbollah also claimed that it shelled Israeli soldiers in Taybeh, Marjaayoun District, causing casualties.[97] A Hezbollah-aligned source claimed on March 10 that IDF tanks had begun advancing toward Taybeh from Aadaysit Marjayoun and Rab el Thalathine, Marjaayoun District.[98]

**The IDF has continued to conduct airstrikes targeting Hezbollah across Lebanon.** The IDF said on March 14 that it has struck around 110 Hezbollah headquarters since the start of the war.[99] The IDF conducted multiple rounds of strikes targeting Hezbollah infrastructure in Beirut and southern Lebanon on March 13.[100] Footage geolocated by ”Stinky” on X on March 14 shows that the IDF struck a Hezbollah missile depot east of Mjadel, Tyre District.[101] The IDF said on March 13 that it killed a member of Hezbollah’s aerial unit and a leading weapons manufacturer in Beirut.[102] Israeli strikes in Beirut on March 12 also killed Hezbollah communications unit commander and IRGC Quds Force Palestine Corps member Hisham Abdul Karim Yassin.[103] The IDF said that Yassin was responsible for rebuilding Hezbollah’s capabilities and deepening Iran’s presence in Lebanon.[104] Yassin is the fifth Quds Force commander and third commander from the Palestine Corps that the IDF has killed since the start of the conflict.[105]

**The IDF has continued to conduct ground operations in southern Lebanon.** The IDF reported that the 7th Armored Brigade (36th Division) has conducted raids that destroyed Hezbollah infrastructure and eliminated Hezbollah fighters in southern Lebanon over the past week.[106] The 7th Armored Brigade has destroyed “dozens” of Hezbollah sites, including weapons depots, headquarters, and observation posts.[107]

## Other Axis of Resistance Response

**Iranian-backed Iraqi militias continue to attack US forces and interests in Iraq. Likely Iranian-backed Iraqi militias conducted a drone attack targeting the US Embassy in Baghdad overnight on March 13 and 14.** A security official told Western media on March 14 that two drones hit the embassy, causing a fire on the embassy’s roof. The fire damaged a satellite communications system.[108] The *New York Times* reported on March 14 that Iranian-backed Iraqi militia Kataib Hezbollah claimed responsibility for the attack.[109] Likely Iranian-backed Iraqi militias previously conducted a rocket attack targeting the US Embassy in Baghdad on March 7, which US air defenses intercepted.[110] The US Embassy in Baghdad stated on March 14 that all US citizens should leave Iraq immediately due to repeated Iranian-backed Iraqi militia attacks against US citizens and interests.[111]

Likely militia front group Saraya Awliya al Dam also claimed on March 13 that it conducted eight “operations” targeting US bases in and outside of Iraq.[112] The group also claimed on March 14 that it conducted a drone attack targeting an unspecified US base at Erbil International Airport on March 13.[113] An Iraqi media correspondent reported on March 13 that air defenses at Harir Base near Erbil International Airport shot down several drones targeting the base.[114] The Islamic Resistance in Iraq separately claimed on March 14 that it had conducted 27 drone and missile attacks against “enemy” bases in Iraq and the region in the past 24 hours.[115] Iranian-backed Iraqi militias are almost certainly responsible for two drone attacks that targeted the Baghdad Diplomatic Support Center at Baghdad International Airport on March 14.[116] Air defenses shot down the drones.[117]

**The combined force conducted strikes targeting Kataib Hezbollah members in central Baghdad on March 14, likely to disrupt Iranian-backed Iraqi militias’ ability to conduct drone and missile attacks.[118]** The strikes reportedly killed three Kataib Hezbollah members, including a missile commander, and a Badr Organization member.[119] A Kurdish journalist reported on March 14 that the Badr Organization member oversaw missile attacks.[120]

**The combined force has struck additional Iraqi militia targets since ISW-CTP’s last data cutoff at 3:00 PM ET on March 13. The combined force struck an 18th Popular Mobilization Forces (PMF) Brigade base in al Qaim, Anbar Province, on March 13.[121]** Multiple Iranian-backed Iraqi militias control PMF brigades that answer to Iran instead of the Iraqi prime minister.[122] The combined force also conducted strikes targeting two Kataib Sayyid al Shuhada “cultural centers” in Ninewa Province on March 13 and 14.[123] The combined force separately conducted drone strikes targeting four 52nd PMF Brigade sites in eastern Salah al Din Province on March 14, wounding one PMF fighter.[124] The Iranian-backed Badr Organization controls the 52nd PMF Brigade.[125]

**Unspecified actors conducted a drone attack targeting the Lanaz oil refinery in Erbil, Iraqi Kurdistan, on March 14.[126]** The attack caused a fire and forced the refinery to shut down.[127] Unspecified actors separately conducted a drone attack against the UAE consulate in Erbil on March 14, injuring two security personnel.[128] No group has claimed responsibility for either of these attacks at the time of this writing.

## Iranian Internal Security

**Iranian security forces have conducted a series of arrests targeting individuals accused of espionage, gathering intelligence, or conducting other anti-regime activities across Iran since ISW-CTP’s last data cutoff at 3:00 PM ET on March 13.[129]** Iranian media reported the following arrests on March 13 and 14:

- LEC forces in Lorestan Province arrested an individual with alleged ties to Mossad and six other members of an “espionage network” on March 13.[130]
- Iranian authorities arrested four individuals in Robat Karim, Tehran Province, for filming “sensitive” locations and eight individuals allegedly linked to “terrorist groups” in Hamedan, western Iran, for collecting imagery of military and law enforcement facilities.[131]
- Iranian security forces arrested three individuals in Khuzestan Province who had reportedly conducted “armed attacks against security guards and public places” in recent days.[132]
- Security forces arrested 13 individuals and seized three Starlink devices in Qom Province, arrested two cells in Fars Province that had allegedly sent manipulated images to anti-regime media, and arrested two ten-person cells in Khorasan Razavi and Mazandaran provinces for allegedly sharing sensitive information about military and economic sites with foreign intelligence services.[133]
- Iranian authorities arrested 14 individuals who allegedly intended to incite unrest and “chaos” in Kerman Province, southeastern Iran.[134]
- The LEC arrested 54 alleged “monarchist operatives,” likely referring to individuals with ties to the MEK, and seized a variety of small arms and ammunition in an unspecified location.[135]
- Iranian forces seized 54 Starlink devices in Hormozgan Province on March 14.[136]
